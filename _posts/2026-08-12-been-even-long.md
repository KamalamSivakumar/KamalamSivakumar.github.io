## being nice and not a weirdo

This post is inspired by something my amma once said: *"ரக ரகமா மனிதர்கள் இருப்பாங்கடி"* — literally, "people will be of all types," but colloquially, or in context, it means, "weirdos exist."

It's nice to think that people can be so diverse in their thought processes, and that everyone's experiences are their own and can't really be compared. I like to think that every human being has their own character arc, with all its different phases, and yes, sometimes people can have a weird phase too.

I think the weirdest of them all, though, are people who don't realise where their thoughts are stemming from, people who think that only they make the right decisions, or people who think so highly of themselves that they judge someone solely based on what others, especially their friends, think of them.

It takes a lot of effort, and self-awareness, to reel your thoughts in and understand why you're doing what you're doing. Not just in your choices, like why you like a certain food or drink, but also in how you interact with others. We all act differently around different people, but I think it's important to be aware of the fact that we inevitably create opinions about someone or something based on how we describe them or it.

I find people who say bad things about others just because they're feeling unhappy to be particularly weird. Like, your self-awareness and understanding of your own thoughts are so low that you talk meanly about others just to feel better about your own unhappiness. How weird is that?

People can, and do, change, but I think it all depends on their self-awareness. Being unhappy does not give you a free pass to behave mean.  

Life is what we make of it.  

Also, on a side note, being performatively nice, aka fake nice or two-faced is weird too. Try to be genuinely nice to people. As my bestfriend says, and that perhaps is my intended takeaway: *be nice to nice people, and be indifferent to weird people.*
